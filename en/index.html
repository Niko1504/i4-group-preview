Deal documents and cap table

Structured preparation of deal documents and a cap table
A package in 10 working days: term sheet, SAFE, cap table, corporate resolutions and a due diligence checklist.
A cap table shows who owns what in a company. Rows list founders, investors and team members with options; columns show the number of shares and the percentage of the total.

Example: founders hold 80%, an investor holds 15%, and 5% is reserved for hiring. A new investor's stake is not added on top: it comes from the same pie, reducing everyone else's percentage. The table is therefore recalculated after every round.

A fund checks the cap table first: it shows who makes decisions, how much founders will retain after dilution and who gets paid when the company is sold.

Data room and English-language materials

A complete document package for an international fund
Pitch deck, one-pager, data room, cap table and due diligence answers — in English, all in one place.
A pitch deck is a 10–15-slide presentation: problem, product, market, metrics, team and the amount you are raising. It is the first thing a fund opens.

A one-pager is a single page with the essentials and key figures. It goes in the first email, before anyone reads the deck.

A data room is a folder of documents for review: legal documents, customer contracts, metrics, financial model and cap table. The fund gets a link and reviews everything directly, without back-and-forth emails.

Due diligence is a review of the company before a deal. The investor verifies that the numbers are real, contracts are signed, the company owns the product and there is no debt. Our answers to review questions are in the same folder.

A cap table is an ownership table: who owns what before and after the round.

The equity stake and package scope are draft terms for the first cohort. Final figures are set in the contract, not on the website.

What the 12 weeks include

Six workstreams that give your team the documents and numbers needed to talk to a fund. No vague promises of community access or motivational lectures.

01

MVP and metrics

We review what already works: product, first customers and funnel. You need an MVP and at least one paying customer to join. You leave with a metrics table a fund can use to make a decision.

02

24-month financial model

Revenue, costs, hiring and runway. Base, growth and crisis scenarios; the model is delivered as an editable file.

03

Sales

We review channels, pricing and repeat sales. We build a 12-month sales plan with numbers, not promises. This also includes sessions with founder-mentors who have raised funding themselves.

04

Investor materials and deal documents

A 10-slide deck, an English one-pager, a data room and a lawyer-reviewed deal document package in 10 working days.

Term sheet
A 2–4-page summary of terms: the investment amount, equity stake, valuation and investor rights. It sets the framework for the deal; final documents are prepared once the terms are agreed.
SAFE
An agreement where the investor pays now and receives equity in the next round at that round's price. Shorter and cheaper than a conventional investment agreement. If no round takes place, the money is not returned — that is the price of speed.
Cap table
An ownership table showing founders' and investors' stakes, team options and the percentage each receives when the company is sold. A fund cannot assess the deal without it.
Corporate resolutions
Shareholder and board resolutions approving new shares, amendments to the charter and fundraising. Without them, the deal cannot be registered and the funds cannot legally be accepted.
05

Investor access

At least five personal introductions to investors matching your stage and sector. Every meeting is followed by a debrief.

06

Demo Day and beyond

Pitch to angels and funds, followed by 12 months of support: fundraising, hiring and sales.

Frequently asked questions

How many investors participate in a deal?

As many as the team needs. If there are several investors, they join one deal on identical terms: one leads the review and documentation; the others see the same numbers and decide independently.

How can we test the Georgian market?

Three months in Tbilisi without relocating the whole company: a legal entity, bank account, office, workspace for the team and five meetings with potential customers. A report after each meeting. The outcome: whether to enter Georgia and with what offering.

What is IRA?

Investment Readiness Accelerator. The name explains the purpose: in 12 weeks, a team brings its metrics, financial model, deal documents and pitch to a level where a fund is ready to consider the deal. The programme ends with fund meetings and Demo Day — preparation for a round, not a guarantee of investment.

How much does participation cost?

We discuss terms on a call and set them in the contract. We do not publish figures on the website: they depend on stage, cheque size and what the team needs from the programme.

What if we have no revenue yet?

We accept teams with a working product and their first customers: without that, a fund has nothing to assess. If you do not have a product yet, come back when you have your first paying customers.

Who are your investors?

Private investors who invest alongside us, and partner funds. Investors use a standard SAFE document.

Do you guarantee investment after the programme?

No. We prepare you for a round and arrange meetings, but the investor decides. We do not guarantee returns or sell participation in a funding round.

What does access to international funds mean?

We map funds matching your stage and sector, prepare English-language materials and a data room, make personal introductions and support negotiations through to a term sheet. This does not guarantee investment: the fund decides.

What if we are not from Georgia?

There is a separate programme for overseas teams: three months in Tbilisi — legal entity, bank account, office, team and five targeted customer meetings.
